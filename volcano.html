Volcanic Hazard Preparedness

Understanding Volcanic Alert Levels V1-V5

Understanding Volcanic Hazards

Volcanic eruptions occur when magma reaches Earth's surface through vents or fissures. Hazards extend far beyond lava flows, affecting air quality, water sources, and regional climate.

  • Eruption Types: Hawaiian (gentle lava flows), Strombolian (explosive bursts), Plinian (cataclysmic, ash columns to stratosphere)
  • Primary Hazards: Pyroclastic flows (deadliest, 100-450 mph), lahars (volcanic mudflows), ashfall, volcanic gases, lava flows
  • Volcanic Ash: Microscopic glass/shards, abrasive, conductive when wet, collapses roofs, damages engines, contaminates water
  • Warning Signs: Increased seismicity, ground deformation, gas emissions, thermal changes, minor eruptions
  • Climate Impact: Large eruptions can inject sulfate aerosols into stratosphere, causing global cooling for 1-3 years

Comprehensive Volcanic Preparation

  • Risk Assessment: Know volcano hazard zones, understand lahar pathways, identify safe evacuation routes
  • Ashfall Preparation: Stockpile dust masks (N95 or better), goggles, plastic sheeting, duct tape, air filters
  • Home Protection: Reinforce roofs for ash load (10+ cm can collapse weak roofs), seal windows/doors, protect electronics
  • Evacuation Planning: Prepare "go-bags" with essentials, establish meeting points, plan for livestock/pets
  • Vehicle Preparation: Keep fuel tank full, protect air intake systems, have spare air filters, maintain wiper fluid

During Volcanic Activity: Safety Measures

If Evacuation Ordered: Leave immediately - pyroclastic flows travel too fast to outrun, lahars can occur with little warning.

  • Ashfall Protection: Stay indoors, seal ventilation, wear mask/goggles outside, protect electronics from abrasive ash
  • Pyroclastic Flow Survival: Impossible to outrun - seek underground shelter or substantial building if caught in path
  • Lahar Response: Move to high ground immediately, avoid river valleys, bridges may be destroyed by flow
  • Driving in Ash: Avoid if possible (<1mm ash slippery), drive slowly, use headlights, avoid abrupt stops/starts
  • Gas Exposure: Move perpendicular to wind direction, seek high ground (some gases heavier than air), use respiratory protection

Post-Eruption Safety & Recovery

  • Ash Cleanup: Wet ash before removal (creates less dust), wear protective gear, avoid strenuous activity, dispose properly
  • Water Safety: Volcanic ash contaminates water sources - use stored water, collect rainwater carefully, boil if uncertain
  • Structural Safety: Clear roofs gradually (sudden removal can collapse structure), watch for ash accumulation in gutters
  • Health Monitoring: Watch for respiratory issues, eye irritation, skin problems. Seek medical attention for persistent symptoms
  • Agricultural Recovery: Ash can initially damage crops but provides long-term soil benefits. Test soil pH before replanting
