Flood Safety & Preparedness

Just 6 Inches of Moving Water Can Knock You Down

Understanding Flood Risks

Floods are the most frequent natural disaster worldwide, affecting more people than any other hazard. They can develop slowly over days or occur suddenly as flash floods with little warning.

  • Types: Flash floods (rapid onset), river floods (gradual), coastal floods (storm surge), urban floods (poor drainage)
  • Causes: Heavy rainfall, snowmelt, dam/levee failure, storm surge, ice jams
  • Risk Factors: Living in floodplains, areas with impervious surfaces, downstream from dams, coastal regions
  • Hidden Dangers: Contaminated water (sewage, chemicals), electrical hazards, structural instability, displaced wildlife
  • Climate Impact: Rising sea levels and increased precipitation intensity are expanding flood risk areas

Comprehensive Flood Preparation

  • Know Your Risk: Check FEMA Flood Maps, understand local flood warning systems, know elevation above nearby streams
  • Home Modifications: Install flood vents, elevate electrical systems, apply waterproof coatings, install backflow valves
  • Emergency Supplies: Waterproof containers for documents, life jackets, waterproof flashlight, signaling devices
  • Evacuation Planning: Identify multiple escape routes to higher ground, prepare pet carriers, practice evacuation drills
  • Insurance: Standard homeowners insurance doesn't cover floods - purchase separate flood insurance (30-day wait period typically applies)

During Flood: Critical Safety Measures

Immediate Action Required: Move to higher ground immediately when warned. Do not wait for instructions to evacuate.

  • Evacuation: Follow designated routes, avoid shortcuts (may be blocked), never drive around barricades
  • If Trapped: Move to highest level, bring signaling devices, avoid attic spaces without escape routes
  • Driving Safety: "Turn Around, Don't Drown" - 12 inches of water can float most cars, 24 inches sweeps away vehicles
  • Walking Safety: 6 inches of moving water can knock adults down, use stick to check ground stability
  • Utility Safety: Turn off electricity at main breaker if water enters home, avoid contact with electrical equipment in wet areas

Post-Flood Recovery & Health

  • Returning Home: Wait for official clearance, photograph damage before cleanup, wear protective gear (gloves, masks, boots)
  • Water Safety: Assume all water is contaminated until tested, boil drinking water, avoid floodwater contact (open wounds risk infection)
  • Structural Safety: Have professional inspect for foundation damage, mold growth begins within 24-48 hours of water exposure
  • Documentation: Keep detailed records of losses, repairs, and expenses for insurance and disaster assistance
  • Mental Health: Flood recovery is stressful - seek support, maintain routines, recognize signs of depression/anxiety
