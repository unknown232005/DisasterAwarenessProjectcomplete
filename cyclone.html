Cyclone Preparedness Guide

Understanding, Preparing, and Surviving Tropical Cyclones

Understanding Cyclones

Tropical cyclones (known as hurricanes in the Atlantic, typhoons in the Pacific) are massive rotating storm systems that form over warm ocean waters. They are classified by sustained wind speeds using the Saffir-Simpson scale (Category 1-5).

  • Formation: Requires sea temperatures above 26.5°C, moisture, and consistent wind patterns
  • Structure: Eye (calm center), Eye Wall (most intense winds), Rain Bands (spiral bands of rain)
  • Primary Threats: Storm surge (accounts for 90% of cyclone fatalities), high winds, torrential rainfall, tornadoes
  • Season: Typically June-November (Atlantic), May-October (Pacific)

Comprehensive Preparation Strategy

  • Home Safety: Install storm shutters, reinforce garage doors, trim trees, secure loose outdoor items
  • Emergency Kit: 3-day water supply (1 gallon per person daily), non-perishable food, medications, important documents in waterproof container
  • Communication Plan: Designate out-of-state contact, establish family meeting points, download weather alert apps
  • Evacuation Planning: Know your evacuation zone, prepare pet carriers, keep car fuel tank full during storm season
  • Property Protection: Clear gutters and drains, install sump pump with battery backup, elevate electrical appliances

During Cyclone: Survival Protocols

When Warning Issued: Fill bathtubs with water for sanitation, charge all devices, move valuables to upper floors.

  • Shelter Location: Small interior room on lowest level, away from windows. Avoid rooms with external walls.
  • If Evacuating: Leave early, follow designated evacuation routes, never drive through flooded roads (6 inches of moving water can sweep away vehicles)
  • During Storm: Stay indoors, monitor official updates via battery- powered radio, avoid using candles (fire hazard)
  • Eye Passage: If eye passes overhead, remain sheltered - dangerous winds will return from opposite direction

Advanced Safety Tips & Recovery

  • Power Safety: Turn refrigerator to coldest setting before storm, unplug sensitive electronics, use surge protectors
  • Documentation: Photograph property before storm for insurance, keep policy numbers accessible
  • Post-Storm: Wait for official "all clear" before going outside, inspect home for structural damage, avoid downed power lines
  • Water Safety: Boil water until declared safe, beware of contaminated floodwaters containing sewage and chemicals
  • Mental Health: Recognize signs of storm-related stress, maintain routines where possible, seek community support
