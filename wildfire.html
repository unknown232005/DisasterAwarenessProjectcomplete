Wildfire Preparedness Guide

Creating Defensible Space Saves Homes and Lives

Understanding Wildfire Behavior

Wildfires are uncontrolled fires burning in wildland vegetation. Climate change has extended fire seasons, increased fire intensity, and expanded wildfire risk to previously unaffected areas.

  • Fire Triangle: Requires heat, fuel, oxygen. Remove one to stop fire
  • Spread Methods: Direct flame contact, radiant heat, spotting (embers carried up to 1 mile ahead), crown fires (tree canopy)
  • Weather Factors: Low humidity (<30%), high temperatures (>90°F), strong winds (>15 mph), dry vegetation create extreme fire conditions
  • Fire Intensity: Measured by flame length and rate of spread. High-intensity fires create own weather (pyrocumulus clouds, fire tornadoes)
  • WUI Risk: Wildland-Urban Interface areas where homes meet flammable vegetation pose greatest threat to lives/property

Comprehensive Wildfire Preparation

  • Defensible Space Zones: Zone 1 (0-30 ft: lean/clean/green), Zone 2 (30-100 ft: reduced fuel), Zone 3 (100+ ft: managed vegetation)
  • Home Hardening: Class A fire-resistant roofing, ember-resistant vents, tempered glass windows, non-combustible siding
  • Evacuation Planning: "Go-Kit" with essentials, pet carriers, important documents, establish multiple evacuation routes
  • Communication Plan: Designate out-of-area contact, know emergency alert systems, establish family meeting points
  • Community Preparedness: Participate in Firewise USA program, know neighborhood evacuation plans, clear shared access roads

During Wildfire: Survival Strategies

If Evacuation Ordered: Leave immediately. Delayed evacuation causes most wildfire fatalities.

  • Shelter-in-Place: Only if specifically designed fire shelter or substantial building with defensible space. Last resort option
  • Vehicle Safety: Park in cleared area, close windows/vents, lie on floor covered with wool blanket, call 911 with location
  • Outdoor Survival: Find area with minimal vegetation (rock outcropping, plowed field), lie face down covering body with soil if possible
  • Water Bodies: Submerge in pond/lake with breathing device (hose, snorkel). Beware of superheated air/water
  • Smoke Protection: Stay low (cleaner air), use wet cloth over mouth, N95 masks filter particulates but not toxic gases

Post-Wildfire Safety & Recovery

  • Return Safety: Wait for official clearance, wear protective gear (mask, gloves, sturdy boots), watch for hot spots
  • Structural Assessment: Check for hidden embers in attics, under decks, in gutters. Use thermal camera if available
  • Air Quality: Poor air quality may persist for weeks. Use HEPA filters indoors, limit outdoor activity, monitor vulnerable individuals
  • Water Safety: Wildfire can contaminate water systems with chemicals, sediment. Use bottled water until system flushed/tested
  • Erosion Control: Burned areas susceptible to flooding/landslides. Install erosion controls, monitor weather closely
