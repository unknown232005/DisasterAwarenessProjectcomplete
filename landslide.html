Landslide Safety Guide

Recognizing Warning Signs Could Save Your Life

Understanding Landslides

Landslides are mass movements of rock, earth, or debris down slopes. They can occur suddenly with devastating force or develop slowly over time, affecting stability of structures and transportation routes.

  • Types: Rockfalls (free-falling rocks), Debris flows (fast-moving slurry), Earth flows (slow-moving soil), Slumps (rotational movement)
  • Triggers: Heavy rainfall (most common), earthquakes, volcanic activity, freeze-thaw cycles, human activities (excavation, deforestation)
  • Speed Range: From millimeters per year (creep) to over 200 mph (rock avalanches)
  • High-Risk Areas: Steep slopes, areas with previous landslide activity, bases of drainage channels, developed hillsides
  • Warning Signs: New cracks in ground/walls, tilting trees/fences, unusual water seepage, bulging ground

Landslide Risk Assessment & Preparation

  • Property Assessment: Consult geological surveys, identify historical landslide areas, monitor slope stability during wet seasons
  • Drainage Management: Install proper drainage systems, maintain gutters and downspouts, direct water away from slopes
  • Slope Stabilization: Plant deep-rooted vegetation, install retaining walls, use erosion control blankets on bare soil
  • Early Warning Systems: Install inclinometers, monitor crack gauges, establish evacuation triggers (rainfall thresholds)
  • Emergency Planning: Identify safe evacuation routes away from likely flow paths, establish warning signals with neighbors

During Landslide: Immediate Response

If Inside: Move to second story if possible, take cover under sturdy furniture, protect head.

  • Evacuation Protocol: Move perpendicular to landslide path (not downhill), reach high ground if possible
  • If Driving: Watch for falling debris, collapsed pavement, unusual water flows. Abandon vehicle if blocked
  • Warning Signs During Rain: Listen for unusual sounds (trees cracking, boulders knocking), watch for sudden water flow changes
  • Debris Flow Response: These move faster than you can run - immediately move to upper floors or climb sturdy trees
  • If Trapped: Make noise to attract rescuers, conserve energy, avoid inhaling dust if in debris

Post-Landslide Safety & Recovery

  • Stay Away: Avoid landslide area - additional slides may occur, ground may be unstable, hidden cavities may exist
  • Check Utilities: Look for gas leaks, damaged electrical lines, broken water pipes. Report to authorities
  • Structural Assessment: Have professionals inspect foundations, look for new cracks, check for tilting
  • Water Safety: Landslides can contaminate water sources - assume water unsafe until tested
  • Long-Term Monitoring: Continue monitoring area for movement, especially after heavy rains or seismic activity
