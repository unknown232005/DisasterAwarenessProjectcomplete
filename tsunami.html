Tsunami Survival Guide

When the Ocean Retreats, Danger Approaches

Understanding Tsunamis

Tsunamis are series of ocean waves caused by sudden displacement of large water volumes. Unlike wind waves affecting surface, tsunamis involve entire water column from surface to seafloor.

  • Generation: 90% caused by subduction zone earthquakes, also landslides, volcanic eruptions, meteor impacts
  • Speed: Travel 500+ mph in deep ocean (jet speed), slow to 20-30 mph near shore but wave height increases
  • Wave Characteristics: First wave often not largest, wave periods 10-60 minutes between crests, multiple waves over hours
  • Natural Warnings: Strong/long earthquake shaking, unusual ocean behavior (rapid recession or rise), loud ocean roar
  • Global Threat: Pacific "Ring of Fire" highest risk, but tsunamis can occur in any ocean or large sea

Comprehensive Tsunami Preparation

  • Know Your Zone: Identify tsunami hazard zones, know evacuation routes to high ground (>100 feet elevation) or inland (>2 miles)
  • Evacuation Planning: Practice walking evacuation routes, identify vertical evacuation structures, plan for disabled family members
  • Emergency Supplies: "Grab-and-go" bags with water, food, medications, sturdy shoes, whistle, waterproof documents
  • Home Preparedness: Anchor fuel tanks, elevate utilities, install check valves in sewer lines, know how to shut off gas
  • Warning Systems: Understand local warning signals, register for emergency alerts, have battery-powered NOAA weather radio

During Tsunami: Immediate Survival Actions

Natural Warning: If you feel strong earthquake shaking near coast, see unusual ocean behavior, or hear loud ocean roar - EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY.

  • Evacuation Protocol: Move on foot if possible (roads may be blocked), go to high ground or inland, follow marked evacuation routes
  • If No High Ground: Go to upper floors of reinforced concrete building (at least 3rd floor), climb sturdy tree as last resort
  • In Water Survival: Grab floating object, orient feet downstream, avoid obstacles, try to control breathing
  • If Driving: Abandon vehicle if stuck, move to high ground on foot, do not park under bridges/overpasses
  • Multiple Waves: Stay evacuated until officials declare area safe - danger can last for hours

Post-Tsunami Safety & Recovery

  • Return Safety: Wait for official clearance, expect extensive damage, watch for downed power lines, gas leaks, structural hazards
  • Water Safety: Assume water contaminated with sewage, chemicals, pathogens. Boil drinking water until tested
  • Health Precautions: Wear protective gear during cleanup, watch for signs of infection from wounds, get tetanus booster if needed
  • Documentation: Photograph damage before cleanup, keep detailed records for insurance/FEMA assistance
  • Mental Health: Tsunami trauma can be severe - seek counseling, connect with support groups, allow time for emotional recovery
