Earthquake Survival Guide

Prepare for the Unpredictable: Seconds Can Save Lives

Understanding Earthquakes

Earthquakes occur when tectonic plates suddenly slip past each other along faults, releasing accumulated stress as seismic waves. Measured on the Richter scale (logarithmic), each whole number increase represents 10 times greater amplitude.

  • Epicenter vs. Focus: Focus is the underground origin point; epicenter is directly above on surface
  • Seismic Waves: P-waves (primary, fastest), S-waves (secondary, more damaging), Surface waves (most destructive)
  • Aftershocks: Smaller quakes following main event, can continue for days to years
  • Related Hazards: Tsunamis, landslides, liquefaction (soil behaving like liquid), infrastructure collapse
  • Warning Systems: Modern systems can provide seconds to minutes warning based on P-wave detection

Comprehensive Earthquake Preparedness

  • Home Seismic Retrofit: Bolt house to foundation, brace cripple walls, secure water heater with strapping
  • Emergency Supplies: 2-week supply of water/food, wrench to shut off gas, heavy-duty gloves, dust masks
  • Home Safety Audit: Secure tall furniture to wall studs, install latches on cabinets, relocate heavy items to lower shelves
  • Family Plan: Designate meeting locations, establish out-of-area contact, practice "Drop, Cover, Hold On" drills quarterly
  • Structural Knowledge: Know building construction type, identify safest structural zones (doorways in modern homes are NOT safer)

Immediate Response: During Quake

DROP to hands and knees, COVER head and neck under sturdy furniture, HOLD ON until shaking stops.

  • Indoors: Stay inside, move away from windows, avoid exterior walls
  • In Bed: Stay in bed, protect head with pillow (most injuries occur from trying to move)
  • High-Rise Building: Stay on same floor, avoid elevators, don't be alarmed if fire alarms/sprinklers activate
  • Driving: Pull over safely, set parking brake, avoid overpasses and power lines
  • Outdoors: Move to open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights, utility wires

Post-Earthquake Safety & Recovery

  • Immediate Aftermath: Check for injuries, extinguish small fires, listen to battery radio for instructions
  • Building Inspection: Look for structural damage, gas leaks (smell, listen for hissing), electrical system damage
  • Utility Management: Shut off gas only if leak detected (requires professional to restart), turn off water to prevent contamination
  • Aftershock Preparedness: Expect aftershocks, keep shoes and flashlight by bed, maintain emergency kit accessibility
  • Community Response: Help neighbors if safe, avoid using phones except emergencies, follow official evacuation orders
