Tornado Survival Guide

Enhanced Fujita Scale EF0-EF5: Understanding Tornado Intensity

Understanding Tornadoes

Tornadoes are violently rotating columns of air extending from thunderstorms to the ground. They are nature's most violent storms, with wind speeds that can exceed 300 mph in the strongest events.

  • Formation: Require warm moist air, cool dry air, and wind shear (changing wind speed/direction with height)
  • EF Scale: Enhanced Fujita Scale measures damage intensity: EF0 (65-85 mph) to EF5 (>200 mph)
  • Size Range: From few yards to over 2 miles wide, path lengths from short track to over 200 miles
  • Warning Times: Average lead time 13 minutes, but can form with little warning
  • Tornado Alley: Central US has highest frequency, but tornadoes can occur in all 50 states and worldwide

Advanced Tornado Preparation

  • Safe Room Construction: FEMA-approved designs for interior rooms, underground shelters, or reinforced safe rooms
  • Early Warning Systems: NOAA Weather Radio, smartphone alerts, community sirens, understanding watch vs. warning
  • Home Reinforcement: Install tornado-resistant garage doors, secure roof sheathing, reinforce connection between walls and foundation
  • Emergency Supplies: Helmet for head protection, sturdy shoes, whistle, portable charger, important documents in waterproof container
  • Drill Practice: Conduct family tornado drills quarterly, practice getting to shelter within 2 minutes

During Tornado: Survival Strategies

Shelter Priority: Underground shelter > interior room on lowest floor > mobile home > vehicle > open field (as last resort).

  • Best Shelter Locations: Small interior room without windows, under sturdy furniture, protect head with arms/pillows
  • Mobile Homes: NEVER stay in mobile home during tornado - evacuate to substantial building or designated shelter
  • While Driving: Do NOT try to outrun tornado. Options: 1) Drive to sturdy shelter 2) Park vehicle, stay seat-belted, head below windows 3) Lie in ditch if no other option
  • Outdoor Safety: Find sturdy building immediately. If no shelter: lie flat in depression/ditch, cover head, avoid bridges/overpasses (wind tunnels)
  • Warning Signs: Dark greenish sky, large hail, loud roar (train-like), wall cloud, rotating funnel cloud

Post-Tornado Safety & Recovery

  • Immediate Aftermath: Check for injuries, avoid downed power lines, watch for broken glass/nails, use flashlight (not candles)
  • Structural Safety: Leave damaged buildings, have professionals inspect for gas leaks, structural damage before re-entering
  • Utility Management: Turn off gas if smell detected, shut off electricity if water present, avoid using tap water until declared safe
  • Documentation: Photograph damage before cleanup, keep receipts for repairs, contact insurance immediately
  • Community Response: Check on neighbors, avoid disaster area (interferes with rescue), follow official instructions
